# Data Story - Biblioteche Fantasma

The ICCU snapshot contains 19,611 records. The first result is not a count of “closed libraries”, but a taxonomy of conditions: 13,200 records have no special status registered, 1,827 are “no longer existing”, 1,723 “not surveyed”, 1,502 “merged”, 619 “temporarily closed”, and a few others fall under inaccessibility, earthquake-related suspension, partial reopening, or repository without a service point. This distinction avoids turning different administrative phenomena into a single category.

For the main analysis, the project considers 2,497 cases out of 18,956 libraries to be problematic, equal to 13.17%. The phenomenon is nationwide but uneven: Molise and Liguria have the highest regional shares, while the largest absolute numbers are concentrated in systems with more libraries. Milan records 139 cases, Rome 117 and Genoa 90. A high share on a small base and a high count on a large base describe two different aspects of the same phenomenon.

The comparison with 2019–2025 demography introduces an intuitive question: do municipalities losing population have more problematic libraries? The answer is more cautious than a linear narrative. Across 6,659 comparable municipalities with at least one library, the correlation is negative but weak: Pearson -0.106 and Spearman -0.067. Municipalities in decline show a higher average share, but dispersion is wide and the median is zero in both groups. Depopulation may be a context, not a cause demonstrated by these data.

Holdings reveal a second informational limitation. Only 627 of the 2,497 problematic libraries have holdings rows in the secondary dataset. Quantities are often available, but 461 rows are missing. For special collections, no record falls within the problematic scope. This result does not mean that the collections do not exist: it means that the secondary snapshot does not document them for these libraries.

Mergers, by contrast, describe institutional transformation. Of the 1,502 records, 1,412 have a validated target ISIL. The resulting network is fragmented into 410 components, with some targets absorbing many nodes. It is therefore not a “network of closures”, but a map of administrative reorganizations observed in the snapshot.

Finally, the data were transformed into a knowledge graph containing 1,656,407 explicit triples and linked to external resources with nearly complete coverage. Interlinking reaches 100% for libraries toward ICCU and 99.9620% for municipalities toward Linked ISPRA. The project uses public HTTPS URIs under the GitHub Pages namespace, generates Web representations for 19,611 libraries and 7,896 municipalities, and distributes the complete `data.ttl` knowledge graph through a GitHub Release. The published version therefore satisfies all five levels of the 5-star Open Data model, while retaining the limitations of static hosting, such as the absence of full HTTP content negotiation and a public SPARQL endpoint.

The overall story is therefore one of **heterogeneity, coverage and caution**: statuses are not equivalent, geography matters but must be normalized by the denominator, demography shows only a weak association, missing values are not zeros, and semantics must be as precise as the statistics.
